Inspiring others

Sharing what works when AI systems reach production

Most advice on putting large language models into production comes from vendors. I have built systems where a wrong answer moves money, and I share what I learned in three ways. I coach teams inside the organisations I serve. I write and speak in public. I develop individual engineers one to one.

The situation

Few people who have shipped these systems in regulated work say in public what goes wrong. Over 26 years I have built systems where a wrong answer moves money or stalls a dealer network. I have seen teams change how they build only when someone they trust shows them that the safer way works.

What I did

  • At Cox Automotive I walked senior architects and technical leads through the reasoning behind major migration decisions until they owned the decisions themselves. I coached their teams through cloud adoption from 2010 to 2020. Source: Client confirmation available through the named supporter
  • Since 2025 I have built a custom agentic harness at Insight Global that keeps a person in charge and stops short of anything irreversible. I coach the engineers who now run it day to day. Source: Employer confirmation available through the application
  • I write for other engineers on feature flags and on messaging in the age of AI. Other articles cover code quality with Sonar and explainable AI. Source: Writing page, with a link to each article ↗
  • I gave the keynote at the IEEE 5th World Conference on Applied Intelligence and Computing in Jabalpur, and I presented event-driven architecture at IEEE ICCBI in Dubai. Source: Keynote talk page on the conference site ↗ · IEEE ICCBI ↗
  • The human-in-the-loop pattern behind my reconciliation work is published in a peer-reviewed journal, so engineers beyond my own employer can use it. Source: International Journal of Computer Applications ↗
  • I paired junior engineers on the hardest production work and stood behind them while they learned to lead. Source: Named mentees available for confirmation through the application
  • I am guiding an eight-student capstone team at Kennesaw State University, four of them pursuing AI master's degrees, through KnowledgeBridge, a working retrieval-augmented generation platform. Source: LinkedIn honors and awards ↗ · Project documentation available through the application

What changed

The practices outlasted my time on each project. Cox Automotive adopted the canonical data model as its integration standard and still builds on it. At Insight Global, low-confidence matches now stop for a person before they reach the ledger, and the teams I coached apply the same rule to agentic work. The two engineers I mentored longest now lead others. One is a Senior Solutions Architect and the other a Lead Software Engineer at Cox Automotive.

On names. The people I mentored are named in my application, where they can confirm the account themselves.
