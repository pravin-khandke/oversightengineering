Standing in the community

Invited to speak, asked to judge, and read beyond my own organisation

Standing in a profession shows up as other people asking you to do something. The invitations on this page came from conference organisers and journal editors. Each one is listed with a public record where one exists.

Keynote and conference speaking

  • Keynote speaker at the IEEE 5th World Conference on Applied Intelligence and Computing, held in Jabalpur, India on 29 and 30 August. My talk was The Trust Gap: Architecting Autonomous AI Systems for Real-World Accountability, It covered how agent systems fail in production and how to verify them. It also argued that trust should include the sustainability cost of running them. The audience numbered 57. Source: Keynote talk page on the conference site ↗ · Audience figure available through the application
  • Confirmed speaker at Extract Summit, held at Brazos Hall in Austin, Texas on 7 and 8 October. My session is The Naive AI Trap: Why Finance-Grade Extraction Needs Hybrid Agents, on the second day. Source: Extract Summit programme ↗

Technical programme committees

I serve on the technical programme committees of three international venues, where the work is deciding which submitted research is accepted and presented. One of the three is an IEEE workshop rather than a full conference, and I describe it as such.

  • AIIoT IEEE World AI IoT Congress, Seattle, United States
  • SIME Sousse, Tunisia
  • NGSME IEEE workshop, Vilamoura, Portugal

Peer review

I review submitted papers for international conferences, across artificial intelligence and distributed systems as well as the internet of things and cloud computing. The venues are named on the peer review record rather than summarised as a number here, because a named venue can be checked and a total cannot.

Written work

  • Three peer-reviewed publications. One is in the International Journal of Computer Applications and one was accepted and presented at IEEE ICCBI. The third is a co-authored paper for the IEEE International Workshop on Intelligent Systems on event-driven dynamic pricing. They cover human-in-the-loop interfaces and event-driven architectures. Source: Publications, with a link to each paper ↗
  • Sustained technical writing for engineers, published on developer platforms and read outside my own organisation, The subjects are architecture and reliability, and the practical failure modes of AI systems in production. I have also published on HackerNoon, where one article has 888 reads. Source: Dev.to ↗ · Hashnode ↗ · HackerNoon reader count available through the application
  • Author of Using AI at Home and School, an open, age-banded guide to children's use of AI, published as a public repository. Source: The repository, publicly available ↗
  • Interviewed by Authority Magazine for its C-Suite Perspectives series, on where to use AI and where to rely only on humans. This is third-party editorial coverage rather than writing of my own, which is why I list it separately. Source: Authority Magazine interview ↗
